Case study / 007

Building Subtitles: a year of photos, captioned like film

42 photos, each with a yellow subtitle burned in: something I said or overheard that week. 5 ways to look through them, and a camera mode that lets your hand do the steering. This page has it too.

2026Design and codeA birthday project

Uses your camera. The picture stays in this browser and nothing is uploaded. An open hand near the top or bottom of the frame scrolls; a pinch held for a second presses.

01

Intro

For a year I kept a habit: 1 photo a week and 1 line that belonged to that week. A thing I said, a thing someone said to me, a thing I overheard. For my birthday I put them all in one place and called it Subtitles.

1,000 people came by in the first 2 days. They could flip through the year as a film strip, scroll it as a feed, drag it around as a table, spin it as a globe, walk it as a gallery, or do all of that with a hand in front of the camera.

02

The words are in the picture

Most photo sites put the caption under the photo. Here the line sits inside the frame, where a film puts its subtitle, burned into the image file in pure #ffff00 yellow. Save a photo and its sentence comes with it.

The data file keeps every line as text as well, and the site has a small function for setting it. If a line starts with a dash, it's a conversation: it splits at each line break and every speaker gets a row of their own in Courier New. Anything else stays one paragraph in Satoshi, the site's typeface.

The stylesheet carries the yellow too, as a subtitle colour for each theme: #ffff00 on the dark page and #d4a800 on the light one.

03

5 ways to look

The same photos, 5 ways. A view builds itself the first time you open it and stays built after that. Switching fades the old view out over 0.1 seconds, waits 2 frames so the new one has its layout, then fades it in over 0.2.

  1. Horizontal

    A film strip, and where the site opens. The photo in focus is in colour at 70% of the screen's width, up to 960 pixels; its neighbours wait in grey at half that. A ticker underneath marks your place in the year.

  2. Vertical

    The same strip stood on end, with the ticker down the left. It runs on the same spring.

  3. Canvas

    Every photo on a surface with no edges: 7 to a row, 280 pixel cards with 200 pixel gaps. Drag it in any direction for as long as you like.

  4. Globe

    80 planes on a sphere. It flies in when you open it, keeps turning when you let go, and zooms from outside the sphere to inside it.

  5. Gallery

    A corridor 110 metres long with the photos on both walls. Walk it with the keys, or swipe and pinch on a phone.

The real thing, recorded Subtitles in its dark theme, moving through the views in the order of its controls.

    04

    With your hand

    Turn on the camera and MediaPipe's hand landmarker finds 21 points on a hand, every frame, in the browser. The site reads 4 of them. The index fingertip is the pointer. The thumb tip against the index tip is the pinch. The wrist and the base of the little finger give a rough sense of how close the hand is.

    A pinch is those 2 tips closer than 0.06, in the camera's own units, where the frame is 1 wide and 1 tall. Hold a pinch for a second without moving more than 60 pixels and it counts as a click. The fingertip is smoothed first, 35% new position to 65% old, to take the shake out.

    Your hand's place in the frame also works like a joystick, with a dead zone of 0.07 either side of the middle. On the globe, pushing your hand toward the screen widens the span from wrist to little finger, and that moves you in.

    On this page. Scrolling uses a dead zone of 0.22 instead of the site's 0.07, so you can point at something without the page moving. A held pinch presses whatever is under the cursor.

    05

    A strip that settles

    The horizontal view never comes to rest between photos. While the wheel is turning, the strip chases where the wheel says it should be, closing 15% of the gap every frame. 150 milliseconds after the wheel stops, a spring takes over and pulls it onto the nearest photo.

    The whole spring is a line of code: vel += (dx * 240 - vel * 30) / 60. 240 is how hard it pulls, 30 is how much it holds back, and 60 is the steps a second it runs at. Those 2 numbers put it just under critical damping, at a ratio of 0.97, so it lands without a bounce you can see.

    The ticker under it is a bell curve. Ticks within 4.5 places of where you are grow, from 21.5 pixels to 68.8.

    06

    A table with no edges

    The canvas lays the photos out 7 to a row, 280 pixel cards with 200 pixel gaps, or 220 and 70 on a phone. Then it draws the whole grid 9 times over, 3 by 3.

    You only ever look at the middle copy. Drift far enough into an outer one and the canvas jumps back by exactly 1 grid. Every copy is the same, so the jump can't be seen. Let go mid-drag and it coasts, keeping 93% of its speed each frame.

    Opening a photo

    Click a photo, on the canvas or the globe, and it lifts out of its place. The card starts at the exact rectangle the photo had on screen, measured from the page on the canvas and projected from the plane's 4 corners on the globe. It grows to 90% of the width over 0.7 seconds, its corners rounding from 3 pixels to 22, and tips back 5 degrees as it arrives.

    Once open, it leans toward your pointer, up to 10 degrees one way and 9 the other. Move off it and it swings back on an elastic ease.

    07

    A sphere from a spiral

    There are 42 photos and 80 planes, so the globe shuffles the photos, uses them all, shuffles again, and stops at 80.

    Each plane's place comes from 2 lines. phi = acos(-1 + 2i/80) moves up the sphere in even steps of height, from one pole to the other. theta = sqrt(80 * PI) * phi winds round it on the way. Even steps in height give every point the same share of the surface, and the winding keeps neighbours apart, like seeds in a sunflower. Switch Fig. 1 to the spiral to see the path.

    On the way in, the camera travels from z = 55 to just outside the sphere in 125 frames, at a steady speed, while the globe grows from 30% to full size. After a drag it keeps 92% of its spin each frame. Zoom runs from z = 30 all the way to 0.5, which is inside the sphere, looking at the photos from behind.

    08

    A corridor 110 metres long

    The gallery is a room 14 metres wide, 5.5 high and 110 long, with concrete on the walls and wood on the floor, both textures from Poly Haven. The ceiling is the same wood, flipped, a mirror of the floor. 24 lights hang down the middle.

    The photos alternate walls. Their sizes come from a sine of each photo's place in the list, 5 heights from 0.68 to 1.95 metres, so the hang is uneven but the same on every visit. A photo's texture loads only once you're within 22 metres of it.

    Click a photo and you walk over and turn to face it from 2.2 metres back, with its line underneath.

    Why 110. Each wall is spaced on its own, and the corridor runs to 110 metres so every photo has room on its wall.

    09

    Underneath

    There's no framework and no build step. Every view reads from a single data file that lists the weeks. The originals are 16:9 PNGs, 1280 or 2560 pixels wide and 136 MB together, so they live on Cloudinary.

    A small upload script finds every entry in the data file without a cloudinary_url, uploads its image under its own id with overwrite turned off, and writes the address it gets back into the file. The page then asks Cloudinary for each photo at the width it's drawn, times the screen's pixel density, up to 3.

    Images are cached for 7 days. The scripts, the styles and the data file are never cached.

    10

    What I cut

    The plan was 4 views and 54 photos. What shipped was 5 views, hand tracking and 42 photos.

    The biggest thing I cut was a visitor card: you could sign the site with a drawn signature, on a card in metal or glass, and see how many people were looking at the same time. I redesigned it twice and then took it out, along with a background image toggle and a gradient accent bar. What's left is only about the year.

    11

    Conclusion

    Subtitles is a small thing with a lot of ways in. Under the 5 views and the hand tracking it's still a year, a photo and a sentence at a time. The work was in making every way of looking fit those photos, and in deleting the parts that were about something else.
