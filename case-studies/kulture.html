Case study / 008

Building KULTURE*: a party with a boarding pass

A one-night street-culture party in Ojo, Lagos, for students coming off exams. The site opens on a ticket you can tilt, sign and switch, and everything after it follows a single idea: the night is a flight.

2025Brand, web and print

01

Intro

KULTURE* was a single night in Ojo, on the Lagos mainland, for students who had just finished exams. Doors at 9PM, 18 and over, room for about 500.

I did the brand, the website and the print. The website was the part that had to turn interest into tickets, and 430 went in 5 days.

The brief gave the site 3 jobs:

Inform guests about the event. Sell tickets. Capture the brand energy and get people hyped.

The page had to sell before every detail was settled, so it leans on what was certain: the name, the look, the prices and the time the doors opened.

The tiers. Early Bird at ₦3,000 for the first week, VIP at ₦5,000, and tables from ₦100,000.

02

The night is a flight

The brand document asked for bold, maximalist streetwear, the asterisk as the mark, and red used only as an accent. It asked for a countdown and a scrolling marquee. It never mentions flights. That idea came in the build.

So buying a ticket became boarding. The pass runs from OJO to PTY, Lagos to Party, and the same pass turns up 3 times: on the first screen, in the footer, and in the print files.

  1. A body and a stub

    A single card, split by a perforation with a notch at each end. On screen it's HTML. In print it's an SVG, 8.5 by 3.5 inches.

  2. OJO to PTY

    Lagos to Party. The 2 codes sit top right, each with its city spelled small underneath.

  3. The passenger

    On the site it reads YOUR NAME until you type yours. The printed pass reads ADMITS ONE.

  4. Gate A1, departs 21:00

    The doors opened at 9PM, so that's when the flight leaves.

  5. Class is the tier

    Early Bird, VIP or Table on the site, where the class is printed in red. Regular, VIP or Table in print.

  6. The code

    A QR and a booking code, KULTURE25 · VIP · OJO, over SCAN TO VERIFY.

  7. The stub

    K*, BOARDING PASS, your class and your name. On the site the stub repeats each letter as you type it.

03

A ticket you can hold

The pass leans toward your cursor on 2 axes. Where the pointer sits on the card runs from −0.5 to 0.5 each way, and that is multiplied by 12, so the most it ever leans is 6 degrees, seen through a perspective of 900 pixels.

Every mouse move starts a new 0.35 second tween with power2.out, quick at first and gentle at the end. When the cursor leaves, a 0.55 second elastic.out(1, 0.5) brings it back flat. It swings past flat and settles.

Then it lets you make it yours. Click the name and type, and the stub repeats it as you go. Switch the tier and the class, the stub and the code along the bottom change with it. Nothing is saved.

Touch. The tilt follows the mouse. Both figures here follow a finger too, with the same numbers.

04

The first screen goes sideways

The first screen is 3 panels side by side on a track 300vw wide, each panel the width of the window: the ticket, then FOR THE KULTURE, then the countdown.

While the page sits at the very top, the scroll wheel moves the track sideways instead of down. At the last panel it lets go and the page scrolls on. On a phone, a vertical swipe moves the track 2.5 times as far as your finger, and a swipe of 15% of a panel is enough to snap to the next one.

The cursor. With a mouse, the site drew its own pointer: a dot, and a ring that catches up with it over 0.55 seconds, inverting whatever it passes over and growing 1.65 times over anything you can click. It's switched back on over the miniature.

05

Words that won't sit still

The second panel says FOR THE KULTURE on 3 lines, and none of them keeps its typeface. Each line steps through a list of 10 font stacks on its own timer: the first every 120 milliseconds, and 23 more for each line after it, so 143 and 166. Each line also starts 40 milliseconds after the one above.

Because the timers differ, the lines drift in and out of step. The cycle runs only while the panel is at least 30% on screen, and when it stops every line goes back to Futura.

06

A departures board

The third panel counts down on 8 tiles, 2 each for days, hours, minutes and seconds. Every tile keeps track of its own digit, so only the tiles that change ever move.

Each tile is half a split-flap. When its digit changes, the new number is printed underneath straight away, top half and bottom half. A single flap, still showing the old number, folds down from 0 to −90 degrees in 0.28 seconds with power2.in, slow and then fast, until it's edge-on to you and disappears.

This board counts down to the next 9PM in Lagos, the time the doors opened.

07

A marquee that hears you scroll

Under the first screen, 3 bands cross the page at −6, 5 and −6 degrees: OJO, LAGOS, DJ SETS, VIP SECTION, AMAPIANO, AGBADA OR SNEAKERS. They move every frame, by 1.4, 1.1 and 1.7 pixels, and the middle one goes the other way.

Scrolling pushes them. On each scroll event the site divides how far the page moved by the time since the last one, and sets a boost of 1 + min(speed × 6, 8). Every frame the boost then loses 5% of whatever is left above 1.

The whole site in 30 secondsRecorded on a desktop, top to bottom.

    08

    The lineup flies in

    THE EXPERIENCE is 6 cards: red carpet, photo activation, DJ sets, VIP section, Lagos vibes and limited entry. On a desktop the section pins to the screen, and the cards fly in from alternate sides as you scroll.

    Each card starts shifted 130% of its own width to one side, turned 14 degrees. On a timeline 5.5 units long, a new card sets off every 0.9 and takes 1 to land, so each one is still arriving when the next leaves. Each lands a little more turned than the last, 1.5 degrees plus 0.4 for every card before it, so the pile ends up uneven.

    The timeline is tied to the scrollbar with a scrub of 1.5, so the cards take a second and a half to catch up with the page. Under 768 pixels none of this runs. The cards stack and fade in.

    09

    Off the screen

    The pass also went to print, as code. A Node script draws each tier as an SVG 8.5 by 3.5 inches, then Puppeteer exports a PNG at 3 times the size, 2550 by 1050, and a single PDF with a page per tier.

    Regular is cream, VIP is black and Table is red. In print the passenger line reads ADMITS ONE, and the stub carries a barcode instead of a name.

    A backdrop from code

    The step-and-repeat was code too. A Python script takes the 7 partner logos, strips their backgrounds and turns them white. A Node script lays them out: rows of logos offset like brickwork, a thin strip of repeating text over each row, and an oval fade behind the wordmark. Any logo that lands in a box round the middle, 72% wide and 28% tall, drops to 7% opacity.

    It writes 3 colourways at 3 sizes, 8 by 8, 8 by 10 and 10 by 8 feet, as SVG, a PNG at twice the size, and a PDF at full size. Changing a logo or a size is a rerun.

    10

    Conclusion

    What holds KULTURE* together is a single idea, used everywhere. The night is a flight, so the ticket is a boarding pass, the countdown is a departures board, and the tiers are classes. The site didn't describe the party. It handed you a ticket and let you put your name on it.
